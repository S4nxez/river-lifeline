How the radar decides

Calm water reflects the radar pulse away from the satellite and shows up dark. On every pass we map that water, look for a continuous water path from each community's landing to the hub town, and measure the narrowest point of that path.

A community counts as isolated only when two passes in a row show no water route. Places that never show a route the radar can resolve, and places away from any water, are left out of the counts.

The radar sees water, not depth. A channel can still be there and be too shallow for boats, which is why narrow routes are flagged as at risk. Check against river gauges and local reports before acting.

Water under floating plants or flooded forest shows on its own layer: the radar sees the water beneath, but such stretches can be closed to boats, so they are never counted as a route.
